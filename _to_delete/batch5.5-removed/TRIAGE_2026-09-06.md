### 2.2 A laid tile appears, then vanishes; the action bar stays on Lay Track

Seen again after the game auto-skipped from B&O to PRR, in the playtest before #1227 landed.

**Plausibly downstream of #1227, and not provable from here.** With the server's auction unseated, no private
was owned on the server -- so a lay that depended on a private's power (the D&H free station, the C&StL free
tile, a terrain discount) would be legal on the client and refused or charged differently on the server. The
server appends the no-op, the client's optimistic tile is rolled back, and the action bar never advances.
That is exactly the reported shape, but the log for that playtest was not exported so it cannot be checked.

**Re-test on the corrected server.** If it recurs, the alarm names the field and the server window names the
refusal; either is enough.

### 2.4 The Dividends mini-chart animates in the wrong coordinate space

The Stock Market tab is correct. The mini view is not: a rise animates five cells right; a fall from $90 to
$82 teleports four right, slides one left, then teleports to $82. Reads like par-box coordinates being used
where price-grid coordinates belong (#415's distinction). Cosmetic, and it does not touch the log.

### 2.5 REGRESSION: the "your turn" whistle fires at game start

Should wait until the active player skips or reaches the end of the cinematic intro. Audio-on-mount, not
authority. The intro's tests were rewritten recently (the title card came out, −7 tests) — worth checking
whether the whistle's trigger moved with it.

## 4. UI and UX backlog

Grouped so they can be done in one pass rather than one at a time. None of this is worth touching while the
board can still lie to the player.

### 4.1 Legibility — the one with the widest reach

**"Everything is way too small." One player needed 250% browser zoom.**

The reporter's own screen reads correctly, which is exactly what makes this easy to dismiss. Testable without
their hardware: emulate a smaller viewport and a higher device pixel ratio rather than trusting one machine.
The likely cause is fixed `px` sizing throughout — a `rem`-based scale with one root size responds to the
browser's own accessibility setting, which is what a player at 250% zoom is actually reaching for.

**This is the item most likely to decide whether someone plays a second game.**

### 4.2 Corporation treasury slide-out, replacing the train-purchase toast

Reported: the train-purchase toast **is not registering with players**. Other consequential moments have
stronger, more direct indicators — laying on a terrain hex warns about the cost and treasury effect, a
dividend opens the dividend machine slide-out — and a toast is the weakest signal in the vocabulary.

**The change:**

- **Drop the train-purchase toast.** Keep the depot-supply one.
- **Add a treasury slide-out** for any corporation treasury adjustment, mirroring the player-cash slide-out.
- **Position: top right, below the Action Bar** — deliberately not the bottom left, which is where player
  cash lives. A corporation is not a player and should not share its corner.
- **Same animation vocabulary**, but a **subtraction-merging** variant is needed; the existing one only
  merges by summing.
- **Sound: undecided.** Reusing the player-cash cue may over-associate the two.

### 4.3 Private Company payout modal

- **(a)** Print player names **in their player colours** rather than a colour swatch beside the name.
- **(b)** Drop the rules line at the top (*"Paid from the bank at the start of every Operating Round…"*) —
  the modal appears at the moment the rule fires.
- **(c)** **The sizing is backwards.** The payout figure (`$15`) is larger than the cash consequence
  (`$new > $old`), making the most important number on the panel the least legible. Invert.
- **(d)** **Make the "Also collected" rows expandable.** Collapsed: player name and cash consequence, in
  their player stripe. Expanded: that entity's private company holdings and each company's income.

  This supersedes the earlier "full player/corporation card for every collector" idea. The attraction of that
  version was real — private ownership is otherwise buried at the bottom of the screen — but full cards
  would flood the modal. **Expandable rows get the same information without the clutter**, and the collapsed
  state stays scannable.

### 4.4 Chrome and layout

- **Square the tab viewport edges.** The rounded leftmost tab reads as disconnected from the panel it opens.
- **Stock Market tab: splay corporation tokens horizontally, not vertically.** Two reasons, both good: they
  stop covering the cell value, and hovering currently "lifts" a token *underneath* the one above it, which
  is worse than no hover effect at all.
- **Screen flash on "Host Game."**
- **The intro's final "Powered by Neta DAO" hold is ~20% too long.**

## 5. How a bug gets diagnosed here

Export the log with `Ctrl+Shift+L` (host tab), then:

```powershell
cd C:\Users\Bradshaw\Documents\GitHub\1830Juno\server
node dist/server/src/replayCli.js path\to\your-log.json
```

The log carries the whole game. What it cannot carry is **intent** — so note what was clicked and what was
expected. For a divergence, the console's `fields:` list is worth more than either.
